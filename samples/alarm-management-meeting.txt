Meeting: Alarm list redesign – kickoff
Date: (sample, fictional)

Maria Keller (Product Owner): Okay, thanks everyone. So the reason we're here: the alarm list in the operations console. We keep hearing from customers that operators are drowning. During a plant upset they get two, three hundred alarms in a few minutes and they can't tell what matters.

Tom Becker (Lead Engineer): Yeah, and technically we just show everything in time order. There's a priority field but it's a tiny coloured dot in the third column.

Maria Keller (Product Owner): Right. Aisha, you did the site visits last month?

Aisha Rahman (UX Researcher): Yes, three sites, two water treatment plants and one pharma. The big thing: operators told us they scroll past critical alarms because the screen is flooded with low-priority stuff. One shift lead said, quote, "I acknowledge everything just to make the noise stop, then I go and look at the trend screen." That's scary.

Jonas Weber (UX Designer): So acknowledging is basically being used as a mute button.

Aisha Rahman (UX Researcher): Exactly. And another one said "I need to know what changed in the last five minutes, not the last five hours."

Tom Becker (Lead Engineer): We could group alarms. The backend already has the asset hierarchy, so grouping by area or by equipment is possible. Shelving is harder, we don't have that in the data model yet.

Maria Keller (Product Owner): Let's not promise shelving for this release. I think the scope should be: better prioritisation, grouping, and a faster way to get from an alarm to the context, like the trend or the faceplate.

Jonas Weber (UX Designer): What about acknowledging in bulk? People will ask for it.

Maria Keller (Product Owner): I'm nervous about bulk acknowledge. It makes the mute-button problem worse. Can we park that and ask compliance?

Priya Nair (Compliance & Safety): For pharma sites, any acknowledge needs to be attributable, so user and timestamp in the audit trail. Bulk acknowledge is allowed under the standard if each alarm is still logged individually, but I'd want a confirmation step for high priority. I need to check ISA 18.2 guidance on this, I'll come back.

Tom Becker (Lead Engineer): The audit trail already logs individual acks, so technically fine.

Maria Keller (Product Owner): Okay. Decision for now: no shelving in this release. Grouping by asset area is in. Priority must be way more visible. Bulk ack is open until Priya confirms.

Jonas Weber (UX Designer): Do we know how many alarms is typical versus peak? I'd like numbers for designing the list density.

Tom Becker (Lead Engineer): I can pull telemetry from two pilot customers. I think normal is maybe 20 to 40 active, peak during upsets can be 500 plus. I'll confirm.

Aisha Rahman (UX Researcher): Also, a lot of operators are on large wall displays in the control room, but supervisors check alarms on tablets when they're walking the plant. We didn't really design for tablets before.

Maria Keller (Product Owner): Tablet is nice-to-have for this release. Desktop and control-room displays first.

Jonas Weber (UX Designer): And accessibility, the colour dot is a problem for colour-blind operators. We should use shape or text plus colour.

Priya Nair (Compliance & Safety): Agreed, and don't rely on colour alone for priority, that's also in our internal HMI guideline.

Maria Keller (Product Owner): How would we know this worked? Leadership will ask.

Aisha Rahman (UX Researcher): Time to find and act on the highest-priority alarm during an upset. We could measure that in usability tests with a simulated upset. And maybe the number of standing alarms, the ones nobody deals with for hours.

Tom Becker (Lead Engineer): We log ack timestamps, so time-to-acknowledge by priority is easy to get from telemetry.

Maria Keller (Product Owner): Good. Timeline: we want a design we can test with operators in about six weeks. Engineering starts the grouping backend in parallel. Jonas, you own the design. Aisha supports with testing. Tom, the telemetry numbers by next week please. Priya, the bulk ack answer.

Jonas Weber (UX Designer): One more thing, who signs off on the final design? Last time we had surprises from the customer advisory board.

Maria Keller (Product Owner): I sign off, but let's do a review with two customers from the advisory board before we finalise. I'll ask Stefan from the customer success team to set that up.

Maria Keller (Product Owner): Okay, I think that's it. Thanks all.
